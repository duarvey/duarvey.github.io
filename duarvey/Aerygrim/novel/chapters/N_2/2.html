Wellstone Village

Chapter 2

An hour before daybreak, the young traveler finally reached the village of Wellstone, a secluded settlement deep within the forest. Despite its remote location, the village sat comfortably beyond the monsters' usual hunting grounds. But with the newly opened forest path, it revived its long-forgotten role as a waypoint towards Lefora town. What was once a quiet settlement now welcomed a steady stream of travelers and adventurers, making the place safer. A busy street for a humble settlement. Children darted between the crowds, their laughter rising above the merchants' cries for passing travelers. They competed against one another to make their stalls heard. Weapons and armor here. Take a gander, gents; these prices would skyrocket in Lefora.” “Over here if you need potion, freshly made no bitter taste!” “How much is for this fire scroll?” “Sandwich! Who want sandwich! For only 15 yels a piece!” Some adventurers stopped to look at the weapon stall. Blades glinted under their scrutinizing eyes. Some mages went for potions at the opposite stall manned by a witch with her signature pointed hat. Others stocked up on provisions and supplies. The traffic stalled the carriage as they waited for the crowds to clear up. It was almost like a festival. At last, the sound of civilization. Chaos could break out in a village without proper guards. But thanks to knights stationed here and there, the public order was rather manageable. Xen pulled his horse and wove their way through the crowds. His gaze jumped around to places that caught his interest. As pleasant as it might be to lose himself in the refreshing scenery, he had other priorities. So, he took mental notes on the stalls and stores he would visit later as he checked the signboards hanging on every establishment. There! A signboard with the carvings of a mug and moon. He stopped on his tracks and scanned the surroundings for something he could tie his horse to. A man walked towards him and grabbed the reins. “I’ll take from here, lad.” He said. Xen stared at him in silence, eyes narrowed suspiciously. “What are you doing?” “Uhhh.. Are you not going inside?” The man asked, confused. “Yes, I’ll get a room first, then find a stable. I was just looking where I can tied the reigns.” “Sir. We have a stable at the back…” The man trailed off, looking at him suspiciously. “Is this your first time entering an inn?” He blinked with covert surprise and stifled a smile. “…With a horse, yes… You’re the ostler?” The man nodded. Xen covered his face with his palm briefly and asked, “How much?” “It’s 10 coppers per day.” The young man handed over the exact amount, and the ostler took the reins. The horse neighed in protest, so he gently patted its forehead to calm it down. “Behave now, Mr. horse.” He turned to the ostler and said. “Take care of him.” The ostler nodded and led the horse away. Now that was taken care of, he turned on his heel and made a beeline for the swinging doors of the inn. Only a few tables were occupied with adventurers enjoying their breakfast. Some were just having a light chat. Well, it was morning after all; most adventurers were already en route to their quests. And it seemed some were still in the middle of picking their favored quest, like this one party assessing the notice board filled with quest papers. As he reached the counter where an old man stood guard, he asked, “I need a room.” “Just the room? No meals?” The man grunted, sparing a single glance before returning to wiping the counter clean. “…lunch, dinner, then breakfast. Just for one day.” “That would be three silver.” Xen placed his coins at the counter, and the innkeeper slid him a key in exchange. “Yours is at the end.” The innkeeper gestured in that direction with his chin. “Thanks.” “Come down on 11 for your meal.” ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ── The room was surprisingly big enough for three people, contrary to the usual inns in villages. But as usual it was mostly empty, with only a bed and a side desk. Perhaps this coat-rack is an improvement. As soon as he hung his hat on the coat-rack beside the door, he went for the bed. He crouched down, He ran his palm over the well-fixed bed sheet. No dust. He gave it a sniff; he smiled in approval. “It’s freshly washed. Good.” He lifted the mattress and assessed the frame. No tiny creatures. “Good job, innkeeper.” The young man nodded as the bed was spared from the purge. Some inns don’t care after all, so he took matters in his own hands. But this one is a five-star among the inns, village edition. He opened the window to let the fresh air in. A cool breeze welcomed him warmly, unlike the certain innkeeper. But he had a decent place to make up for it. His eyes scanned over the courtyard, which doubled as stables. His horse seemed to be well-behaved with the ostler. Aside from horses, it also housed a few carriages. This was a lucky find considering the number of travelers in the village. After breathing a mouthful of wellstone air, he turned back and removed his coat. He tossed it skillfully on top of the coat rack before throwing himself into bed. “Let’s see here…” he said, looking at the ceiling. I still have some high-grade potions. So I won’t need it now. My priority right now is food and weapons. “Hmm… weapons… should I really do it?” he asked himself again. It’s been on his mind ever since Welma told him about it. A weapon power, too important to pass up. The relic of the dungeon grants an unimaginable growth to one’s prowess. Perhaps any person with considerable strength will go for it. Try their luck no matter how abysmal it is. Compared to some isekai protagonists in the manga he read, he’s aware he had a fair chance of winning compared to other parties. Even if I fail, I can still learn something from it; maybe I can reach enlightenment. His journey might seem aimless, but actually he does have a destination. Not a place, but a state of one’s existence. To reach it, he was not averting any dangers that may come his way. He traversed the unfamiliar paths and fought monsters that even a common adventurer couldn’t take. He faced those hardships in the hope of searching for things he was not aware of. And now an opportunity once again presented itself. But not something he could venture into outright. “Ugghh, dad! Why didn’t you just tell me!” He grunted as he sat up, massaging his temples. He breathed deeply to reset his temperament. Closed his eyes and searched for the answer once more. The answer to the puzzle his father left him when he left the gates. [Never enter the dungeon without reaching it.] ...advice?...warning? Just what are you trying to make me do? But one thing is for sure: his father won’t give him something without a reason. And this uncertainty put the idea of entering the dungeon out of his mind. But he wanted to go. His heart told him he should go for it! Now a chaotic battle raged in his head. Logic vs instinct. Should he go or not? Xen sighed and closed his eyes more. It didn’t take long before he fell asleep. ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ── After having his lunch, he went to the village square, where most stores are located. The weapon shop was the first in the order. He had already listed the establishments in his head beforehand, so he knew where to go. He entered the building with the signboard — with an emblem of a pair of swords crossed behind the shield hanging by the canopy. The rustic smell of blades filled the room. Heavy boots thudded against the wooden board with every step. Adventurers crossed the hall, inspecting the weapons on the display rack. Bows, axes, swords, and utilities such as ropes and torches- a store truly dedicated to the adventurers. He turned to the nearest rack. He feasted with his eyes on the level craftsmanship of the swords. His eyes shifted to the price tag. Yep, Good quality, but bad on the wallet. Perhaps the same could be said for the rest of the weapons on the display as he went for the bundles of swords in the barrel. Xen grabbed one of the swords. He ran his fingers along the flat side of the blade, crawling with scratches. There weren’t any dents so far. “Not bad, I can work with this. How much is this?” he muttered before his eyes fell on the price tag at the edge of the barrel. “150… Alright, Ill take it.” He carefully selected ten swords of similar states and put them on the floor for now. He searched once again for additional items that may seem useful. “Smoke bombs… I’ll take this too.” “And this is… monster repellant… trash.” Xen looked again and went for the bow and arrow, which wasn’t inside the display case. A trick he learned to get cheap items that can be improved with adept hands. As long as it’s fixable, it was a good deal. He took a good look at the inventory one last time before nodding in satisfaction. He waved his hand, and as if on cue, the items on the floor floated and carried themselves beside him; several mages turned his way as he walked towards the counter. “All of this, sir?” The store clerk asked, surprised. “Yes.” He left the weapon shop, leaving the stirred customers. His next task was procuring the food. He went around the villages looking at the signs again. From snacks, breads, cooked dishes, and raw meat. With the gold received from Mr Rhodis. He had much leeway to splurge in large quantities with the privilege of asking for discounts. Once everything had been accounted for, he visited the plaza to take a break. Trimmed gardens and clean pathways, along with the food stalls, drew the tourists. Some couples rested on the other benches, and some folks loitered around on the pathway as kids horsed around on the even carpet. On one of the benches, Xen chewed on his freshly cooked barbecue alone. He gazed at the ochre-tinted sky. A considerable amount of time was spent shopping, so he rewarded himself with simple leisure. He brought out the newly purchased newspaper from the void and read the contents. Though Welma and her friends gave him a heads-up of the events that happened on his camping days, it made him realise how much he missed out. So this newspaper is considered a premium item for him, who usually camped for a long time in remote locations. “Hmm… all the news is focused on the dungeon huh.” Pages flipped every 10 seconds with an occasional bite of his barbecue. It didn’t take long before he finished it all just as he took his final bite. “Now what do I do?” he muttered as he slumped on the bench, staring at the sky as if the answer were hidden on the transparent clouds. Like the cycle of the sun, the question he had been putting off came back again. Xen sighed. He breathed in deeply and exhaled the lingering cheerless vibe. “Alright, let’s organized- what do I get from this?” Headache… that first thought came to his mind as he agonized over his indecisiveness. He went ahead and purchased several without a secured decision. But in case he did, then he had what he needed. A choice that can go either way. Aside from the warnings of his father, he also has to take into consideration the time he will spend in the dungeon. Some take months at a minimum. At worst, it would take a year. Depending on the mechanism of the dungeon, it might even trap the adventurer there without a way out. “In four months, I will enroll in the CC academy too. If I spent my time there for a long time, I’m screwed…” However, the reward is the precious relic that adventurers died to get their hands on. Xen closed his eyes and sighed for the umpteenth time. His eyes cleared of doubt. He smiled, not of victory but of surrender. “… this is impossible. Alright GG.” He got up from the bench and stretched his arms. “Well, At least I have my supplies.” ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ── “Here your food.” “Thanks.” A simple dish of steak and bread, coupled with a glass of fruit juice. A humble dinner, but for the young man, it was a grand celebration for clearing up his mind. Xen began cutting the steak and took a bite. “Excuse me…” “…” Xen swallowed his food; he elegantly arranged his utensils in an arrow shape on the plate and fetched his handkerchief from his coat to wipe off his mouth. His movements were smooth and practiced. He looked forward at the priestess and the swordsman on the opposite side of the table. A youthful couple who seemed the same age as him. They didn’t have any food before them and were only discussing trivial things earlier. “Yes, what is it, priestess?” "I can't help but ask, are you a mage?" “In a way… yes.” Xen looked sideways before nodding as if convincing himself. The priestess beamed and leaned forward. “Then, would you like to join our party? We’re actually going to Lefora to register.” “Sorry guys, I don’t plan to become an adventurer. I’m just a traveler.” Xen smiled wryly. “I… see… s-sorry.” The priestess bowed dejectedly. “But you can find plenty aspiring ones on lefora, right? They’re flocking the town at this moment.” “Yes, that’s actually what we planned first, but I think we need to get to know each and improved our team work before we could take on any quest. So we planned to get members as early as possible. To work on it right away.” “We can increase our chance of winning this way. Even a little.” The swordsman wedged in. ”That’s a nice idea.” Xen smiled. “You never know what will happen within the forest now that the dungeon is there. Teamwork is very crucial.” “They said more high-ranking monsters lurk in the mid region. That’s why we really have to take extra careful.” The swordsman said. “We’re…” The priestess looked at her partner, and the swordsman looked at her, puzzled. “ I’m actually nervous. We will finally debut as adventurers, and the quests are more dangerous than they used to be.” The priestess’s worried gaze lowered. But is that really a problem? Xen thought for a moment and offered the easiest solution. “Perhaps you can first try quests inside the city. To ease into it. You don’t need to face the forest on the outset.” “Like cleaning sewers or finding lost cats. My master didn’t beat me up with a sword for things like that. For the effort, it won’t feed us.” The swordsman crossed his arms as he rant. “Besides, this is actually an opportunity for us rookies. With the forest as of now, we don’t have to go too deep to hunt monsters; we can easily escape and ask for help if things go bad.” The Swordsman explained, or rather, it sounded like he was convincing the priestess. “So hunting monsters it is. But your priestess is somewhat hesitant. Will you guys be alright?” “No! I’m nervous, but I can fight… we’re not afraid. I’m just worried if we can succeed in our first quest or if we can still get along with our members if we fail…” The priestess trailed off, but soon found her words to continue. “ But we have to try. If we can’t, we won’t find out if we have what it takes to be an adventurer. It’s a gamble that’s worth the risk. If we can fight off the monsters in the shallow area, we can develop our teamwork. Little by little, we will work our way to go deeper. I’m not hesitant; I’m just afraid of becoming a failure.” “Oi, I told you that won’t happen. We’ll form a party remember. We’ll go big, and people will sing praises for us.” The swordsman said; his eyes seemed to dream of the day he and his party would be successful. “We don’t have to worry about such things when we haven't even found a party yet. We can worry about it later but first, we need to find members.” “Right! Sorry…” The priestess eased up. It seemed she found her courage. Perhaps they could do this. After all, only those willing to take risks go far and wide in this world. What about Xen? Is he afraid of the risk? No, he wouldn’t be charging into dangerous places if he were. Then about his father’s warning? Wait, is it really a problem? To begin with, he doesn’t know why he shouldn’t go to the dungeon without reaching enlightenment. He was stronger than the average adventurer prowling the dungeon; why can’t he? What’s in the dungeon that you’re so worried about, Father? Maybe, just maybe… …is he just trolling me!? There’s a chance. He did it several times after all. No. Not this time; something was serious about it. His instinct tells him it wasn’t just trivial teasing of a father. What is the answer to the puzzle? “Dungeon… what’s in a dungeon?” he muttered aloud. “Uhhmm, are you alright?” The priestess asked. “I’m fine…” Xen answered. He shook his head as if to shake away his thoughts. But he didn’t get the chance, as the swordsman brought it back. “Are you going to the dungeon by chance?” The swordsman asked. Xen sighed in defeat and answered, “I was… But my father warned me not to go.” “Of course he would; dungeons are dangerous.” “Right, I wouldn’t even dream of entering at our current strength. It’s suicide.” Xen looked at the two, then asked. “What if you are Rank A adventurers, and you are more than capable of conquering a rank C dungeon on your own? But S-rank said you can’t because it’s dangerous. Will you still go to the dungeon or not?” The swordsman pondered for a moment and said, “Isn’t that obvious? Of course we will go. It’s a free reward.” “Right I will go as well.” The priestess supported. “So you will ignore the warning of an exalted rank-S? There might be something in there so dangerous that Rank-S has given you advice not to go.” “Then you change your goal then,” the swordsman said, “Instead of conquering it, your mission now is to find out why Rank-S said that. And there’s only one way to find that out.” “Yes, if we have the power of a Rank A, then we can escape easily. We’re not there to fight we just want to know it. If we investigate, we could plan our strategy to take it down.” “It’s what any adventurers do.” Hearing their answers, Xen rested his forehead on his palms. Right… How can I be so stupid? The solution to the problem is right in the problem all along. Isn’t that how scholars solved a problem? He seethed with rage and frustration at how he missed something so simple. But in the swirling of negative emotions, there is happiness. It may not be the answer he was looking for, but it provides a way to find the answer. Courage to the newly forged resolve and excitement about things he will find inside the underworld. I can worry about school when I get there. With this, his mission to the dungeon is now set in motion. “If you don’t try, you won’t find out, huh…” Xen muttered, barely audible. Then a smile crept between the gaps of his palm. Seeing him frozen on the spot, the priestess lowered her head and tried to peek through his palms. But before she could take a closer look, Xen dropped his arms to the table, startling her. “Alright, good luck to both of us. Thanks for the answer.” Xen said and nodded. “Oh you going to the forest too? Then…” The swordsman said “Yeah, sightseeing.” He took a bite of that steak. “After that, I’ll do some unregrettably stupid things.” ‘Huh…?” the priestess tilted her head quizzically. “Then why do we go together? You’re alone right, its best to have company in the road.” “Sorry to intrude your conversation But we have to speak with kid right here.” A man with a dandy smile stood by their table. He had slicked-back brown hair, and a scar ran through his cheek. His well-defined muscles threatened to break free from his tank top underneath the open jacket. He looked like a typical roughneck leader you see in an alleyway. But there was an unmistakable commanding aura behind this man. The two adventurers broke a sweat at the presence of such a man. But Xen just stared at the man in the same manner he did to anyone. Breaking the suffocating silence, he asked, “Oh, so to what do I owe the pleasure?” The man unceremoniously took a barrel chair beside him and sat down. “My juniors were on a quest… You see, something happened. An accident. They ended up losing some of the horses.” He smiled, but his eyes narrowed dangerously. “One of which is the one you ride to this village.” “I see…” unbothered by such provocation. Xen asked back. “So that broken carriage was theres?” The man didn’t answer but asked instead. “Is there any corpse left behind?” “Only there’s blood and guts littered everywhere. By the looks of it, it seems from direwolves.” He answered truthfully. “Were there any remains or traces of someone’s personal effects? Clothes or whatever from that carriage?” “The carriage was totally obliterated. I don’t see any traces of a person in there. I found the horse deep in the forest. No one seems to be around, so… finders keepers.” “…” The man gazes at him as if he can bore through his thoughts. But the young man remained unbothered and offered, “You want it back? I deserve a little something for it.” “No… keep the horse. Thanks for your time.” The man said as he placed a gold coin atop the table before leaving. He left abruptly as he arrived, leaving the priestess confused. “Was that all about?” “That’s no ordinary adventurer.” The swordsman said, his gaze still tense Xen drank from his glass and looked at the swinging door where the man had just walked out.
