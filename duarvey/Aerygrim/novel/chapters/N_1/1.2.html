The Traveler [Part 2]

Chapter 1

“Are you sure you’re alright up there, sire. There’s still room inside,” Mr. Rhodis asked from the driver’s seat. Reins in his hand, eyes fixed on the road. The carriage rocked slightly against the fading path. Covered with grass. The lack of human intervention rendered the path unhospitable, with the grass crawling all around. Nevertheless, it remained traversable. Give it some time, and the passing carriages will clear it away. “Yep, the weather is good, plus it would be hard for me to respond if I’m inside the carriage.” The young man replied from the roof with his hair jostled by the wind. “I see,” Rhodis said wryly. A needless worry. After all, the person in question can easily maintain his balance. The same could be said for the adventurers in the front, sitting on rope-secured luggage, Welma and Gunther. With the healing potion along with his innate healing magic, Gunther recovered well enough to fight properly. Whereas the mage, Mili, despite being completely healed by the healing potion, remained inside since she was in charge of protecting the family. Her presence inside made Rhodi’s family relatively safer, and she could have ample rest. No monster was nearby at the moment, but that didn’t give adventurers the reason to drop their guard. Perhaps because of what transpired earlier, Welma and Gunther never seemed to intend to shift their gaze away from the trees. Same with Kroft, scanning the surroundings beside Rhodis. The young man removed his mask, making the most of the breeze that brushed past him. A feeling he often indulged in when sprinting, but a different experience when the legs weren’t working. “Hmm?” Welma’s eyes fixed on him, widening with curiosity. Was she mesmerized by his good looks? After all, he had a well-defined face and clear silver eyes. His demeanor radiated a concealed majesty. “Needs some food?” The young man asked. Welma’s face just now reminded him of a kid looking at him when he bought a crepe. She snapped out of her daze and waved her hand in denial. “No, no… that’s not it; I just didn’t expect you to remove your mask so easily… I thought you were hiding your identity.” “Do you know me?” “Uhm… No…” “Then, there’s no need to.” “…huh…” Welma stared, giving a side glance. “Oohhh, you reveal your face. Very… uh, nerdy.” “Hey!” “Sorry, a little bit girly. wait… handsome is the right word. Hehehe.” Gunther laughs dryly, scratching his head as Welma stared at him, jaw slack. “Thank you…You both look impressive as well.” “Hmmh-hmms, you know your stuff lad. To tell you truth, all servants of Sinafel always have good and pious faces.” “Yes… I can see that.” The young man smiled. No clouds drifted at the moment, with the sun long past its zenith. Peaceful weather deserved a good chat. This would be no different from when he tread alone in the forest if no conversation followed. And there’s something he was curious about. “You know this road is infested by monster… bandits even. Why in the world did you decided to take this route? you could’ve made out safer with the main road.” For the merchant, the safety of his merchandise is the outpost’s priority. Not to mention, he even brought his family. Just what circumstances forced this party to risk their safety in traveling this path? Whatever it may be, he needs to know it as their escort. “Indeed we could… if wasn’t for bandits in the Ghled mountain, we wouldn’t be taking this risk.” Rhodis replied. “Bandits, huh…” The young man closed his eyes and sighed. “I get what you mean; I just encountered one earlier.” “I’m sure it was an unfortunate encounter for the bandits,” said Welma. “It was a meaningful encounter.” The young man smirked, causing Welma to stretch a concerned smile. “Just when we’re finally through with the wyverns, now we have to deal with those bastards.” Kroft scoffed. “And there bandits here too.. Along with monsters.” “Welp, If those bandits in the Geld Mountain were just your typical bandits. But a lvl 5 mage heard them. I don’t even know what a talent like that doing there. But lots of merchant already got ambushed. We can’t take risk of going to the main road.” If that’s the case, then that changes the situation. If the goblin warlock’s strength can be around level 4, if they encounter a lvl 5 mage, this party is done in the blink of an eye. Perhaps this is the best way for them, or is it… “Well, their good days are numbered. I heard in the Hamdel village that this country’s own relic holder will be dealing with them personally.” Gunther chimed in. “I really hope so, it’s their loss if the merchant stopped their trade again.” Rhodis said. ”…So… you avoided it by taking an even more dangerous path?” The young man asked, looking blankly into space. “Aren’t you guys jumping out of the pan and into the fire?” Even with the lvl 5 mage in question, it’s still considered safer on the main road; there are more chances of travelers who might come to their rescue. Plus, with that kind of threat looming on the road, caravans will naturally form to avoid conflict. They could’ve just joined in. Why didn’t they? There’s more to it. “Hahaha… Well, that would be true. But for us adventurers, monsters are easier to deal with than the humans, lad. Monsters act on instinct; they’ll retreat after roughing them a little. We just… never expected those goblins to have a warlock on them…” Kroft trailed off at the last sentence, smiling wryly. “I don’t know if you know this, but this path is the shortest route to Lefora from the Rentari town on the other side. But because of the dungeon, Mr. Rhodis wants to reach there as fast as possible.” “Oh, there was a dungeon there?” “Huh… don’t tell me… Isn’t that the reason why you’re traveling here as well. To have your shot at the relic?” Kroft looked up. “…No… I’m just traveling normally.” “what…?” “…” He trekked this trail for days with no prospect of a destination, so of course he didn’t know whatever happened outside. The two stared at him in disbelief at his ignorance. His silence confirmed his innocence, so Welma sighed and filled him in on what he missed. “It’s all over the news. It was three weeks ago. A rank B dungeon emerged in the Vilpora forest. A rank B!” she pressed. What do you think will happen to the towns nearby?” Whenever there was a dungeon, adventurers followed. Rallying behind them would be blacksmiths and merchants to supply their needs. A wonderful traffic that would boost the economy. Even more so if the dungeon contained a relic of power. Perhaps it’s unrealistic to say that no adventurer doesn’t wish to have a relic of their own. Optimistic adventurers and guilds across the land will, without a doubt, flood the town to invade the dungeon. “I see, that’s why you guys are in a hurry.” “Right! If anyone gets the relic sooner, the festivity would be over!” Rhodis exclaimed, eyes narrowed with a hint of worry. He pictured the scenario where the town became normal after the dungeon was gone. He internally cursed the goblins that stole their precious time. “There’s probably a lot of ranked S adventurers heading there right now… perhaps the mysterious wyvern slayer was already there.” Gunther wondered, looking far into the space. “That’s unlikely…” Welma lightly shook her head. “If that’s true, the dungeon wouldn’t be standing right now.” “But there’s a high chance that he’ll prowl in too. Who’s a warrior would missed a chance like that?” “Wyvern slayer?” the young man cut in with an inquiring gaze. “Yes!… wow… you really don’t know anything.” The young man just looked down in response. “Just how long have you been here?” Welma leaned in, pressing for answers. The young man’s narrowed gaze wandered and answered, “A month or so… I think…” “Wow, you might as well join the pilgrimage.” Jokingly, Gunther offered. “… I’m happy where I am.” Welma sighed, filling him up once again as if it was her job. “It’s a day before the dungeon was discovered, the villagers of Hamdel discovered a two wyverns corpses in the square by dawn. No one saw who did it. But the action alone told them that something happened in the nest. So when they sent someone to investigate, they found the flocks were gone, leaving only traces of battle.” “Woah… that’s… insane…” “I know right! To think someone is capable of achieving what the the expedition failed to do fifteen years ago.” “Hamdel just prospered after selling the materials from those wyverns. Not to mention the amount of travelers they have. They sang praises for his valor. Even the traveling bards chipped in to spread his tales.” Rhodis said. “It’s still a wonder why a guy like that drifted here. I first thought for he’s after the dungeon, but he did, he’s action doesn’t add up to time it was discovered.” Kroft said. “I told you guys, I bet my fortune that that guy was a relic holder or someone with high status, on a secret mission. That’s why he concealed his identity. And the wyvern was just an afterthought.”, Gunther said, puffing out his chest, sounding confident. Welma huffed with a smug look and offered her own theory. “Hmmh, It’s a good guess. But still think he was really after the wyvern. There’s might be something he really needed them for… like armors or weapons” “For one person you have to kill a lot?” “Maybe he’s poor, he need in cash.” “Now that’s doesn’t remotely add up.” “He lost in casino. It usually happened in highranks1” The two adventurers bickered with one another, trying to defend their arguments. The young man, still gazing at the sky in silence, offered his own theory. Something that brought them to a halt. “Perhaps he’s just traveling normally.” “Huh?” Silence lingered for a moment. Perhaps the words didn’t register in their ears. Or perhaps it did, but they couldn’t process it. Their puzzled response was something that puzzled the young man as well. “What? What are you surprised about?” The young man continued. “I’m aware of my strength, and I’m traveling normally. Surely some skilled others out there had the same idea.” The two seemed unconvinced as they just stared at him. Gunther, on the other hand, had a hint of pity. Perhaps they were looking through the lens of common adventurers. Strong people are often tasked with demanding jobs. Commanders of an order often spend their time in the office rather than the field. The same for the rank S adventurer. Jobs that they didn’t want to do, but they needed to do out of duty. Yes, they would never know the struggles of those people. The young man sighed in surrender. “I just thought maybe he’s too occupied with problems, and want to let loose, traveling, sightseeing. Everyone want to escape the world once in a while. Even those Rank S are still human after all.” With his words, Gunther and Welma opened their eyes wide in daze. Did they get it? Probably, they looked at each other and smiled. Perhaps they finally were convinced by his answer. “It might be shallow, but that’s the most sound explanation.” Mr. Rhodis offered his support. “There are times a man do things without a deeper reason to distract himself from problems. Perhaps the wyvern slayer just want to travel and let loose.” “The most logical explanation was the easiest one. Huh.” Knoft said. “But still, my guess is more right on the money.” “Yeah right.” Gunther still won’t budge and insisted on his argument. Before Gunther and Welma could again start their banter, the young man perked up and warned them. “There’s fight ahead.” As if their switch flipped, their playful demeanor was replaced by honest seriousness. The adventurers focused on their ears. They heard nothing. But they wouldn’t carelessly dismiss his words. In the field of adventure, the stronger ones often have the sharpest senses and instincts. They patiently waited until faint howls mixed with angry shouts reached their ears as they approached closer. At the far end of the path was a carriage surrounded by a pack of large wolves. “Direwolves,” Kroft muttered under his breath. “Let’s stop the carriage for now, Mr. Rhodis,” Kroft said without a trace of unease. “But don’t you think we should help them?” Rhodis asked with concern. “Unwritten rule of adventurers… Something like no interference, unless they asked for it, right?” the young man asked, confirming his hunch. “That’s is right. Those are Direwoles compared to regular wolves their pelt fetch large sum in the market. So we can’t just help them carelessly then ended up ruining the spoils.” “Worse, they could file a complaint, and accused us of stealing. Since they’re not launching the flare, they don’t need help.” Welma added. “I see…” Rhodis surrendered and didn’t argue further. It was a rule strange to him since he wasn’t an adventurer himself. “Plus, our job is your safety; we might drag you to danger if we proceed haphazardly.” Kroft pressed further. Some people might rush to help. But for adventurers, the priority should always be their quest. For this party, that is the safety of Mr. Rhodis and his family. Rushing into battle that they aren’t obligated to may endanger themselves, especially their client. Thus they watched the battle and waited. “…Looks like it’s over,” said Welma. Perhaps the wolves notice their presence as another enemy; they flee the scene, leaving their fallen kin and their enemies behind. Rhodis whipped the reins, and the carriage moved forward. But before the wheels could get any further, the traveler cautioned them to stop. “Wait.” His sharp gaze zeroed in on the trees on the right side of the forest path towards the mountain range. “What is it?” Kroft asked. His hand slowly reached for the hilt. The adventurers seemed to be looking at the forest as well. “Those wolves, they looked to the north before retreating. They didn’t get away from the adventurers, but from something else… Seconds later, in the distance, faint tremors shook the ground, and the horses of the adventurers stirred in panic. Two free horses made a run for it, leaving the confused adventurers behind. The remaining two horses tried to break free from the shackles of the carriage, so another adventurer tried to calm them down. Tremors grew stronger, accompanied by rhythmic thuds of a heavy object. One adventurer, visibly distressed, smacked an object to the ground. A cloud of smoke exploded and covered the area. But not enough to cover the hulking silhouette reaching beyond five meters in height as it emerged from the trees. One swing of those thick and long arms pushed away the smoke along the carriage, toppling it over to the side. The horse still struggled to get up, attempting to break away. The monster’s full frame came into view. It was a bipedal creature. Its ochre fur gleamed with dull light. “Holy… shit! That’s an urzyk!” The words that escaped his mouth were unseemly for a knight of the goddess. “Mr. Rhodis turn the carriage around. quickly!” Kroft cried, dropping his usual calmness. “W-wait! Those adventurers left that carriage. Their might be people in there. Are we just leaving them—?” “That monster is not something we can turn up against. You safety is our priority than them. let’s go!” Carried by the grave tone of his voice, Rhodis fumbled with the reins to turn the carriage around. However, the young man jumped off the roof. “Sir, traveler, get back here! We need to escape.” Welma cried. “What are you doing, lad!? Are you mad!?” Gunther crawled forward in distress. “I’m doing my job.” The young man answered flatly ”There’s still someone inside the carriage. “Wha—wahtt!?” The young man briskly walked as he fixed his summoned mask on his face. He raised his hand and shaped it into a gun. The Urzyk seemed uninterested in the carriage; it turned its head towards the fleeing adventurers in the distant forest. A muffled whistle rang out. Something cut through the air. Its long ears twitched. The urzyk jumped out of the way before the trunk behind it exploded in half. A tree fell with a heavy sound. The urzyk turned sideways —eyes darting at the new prey. “An invisible magic bullet!” “It packs a lot of power too.” Welma and Gunther commented —their gaze tightened with a hint of excitement. It seemed they decided to stop the carriage and watch their warden in action. The young man was strong enough to handle the packs of goblins. But can he handle a monster much stronger than them? That magic bullet was enough proof that the urzyk at least recognized the young man as a threat. The young man fired again, but the beast dodged with surprising agility despite its enormous size. The urzyk roared and dashed towards him. “It’s— it’s— it’s coming here!” Rhodis stuttered a scream. He hid behind Kroft, guarding the door together with Mili. Their weapons braced for the event that the monster ever reached them. Though it was still a wonder what they could even do if that scenario happened. The young man sprinted straight towards the monster, meeting it halfway. The urzyk jumped and swung its claw. The young man dodged to the right and fired magic bullets at its legs. The projectiles dug in, and blood spurted with every hit. The wound was shallow, not enough to impair its movement; nevertheless, it inflicted a painful response. The urzrk roared and swiped its arm. Its claws gleamed in red light. Danger! The young man jumped high, evading the red arc of energy that cut down several trees in the background. Above, the young man fired again while a sword spilled from a small portal summoned to his right hand. The monster raised its arm, blocking the projectiles. Mana particles erupted from its arm along with bits of flesh from every shot. As the young man landed, he burst forward, indifferent to the series of heavy thuds from the falling trees. The urzyk brought its arms down as the projectiles stopped. —only to realize the human was already in front of its face. The young man thrust his sword without hesitation. The blade pierced its head, and blood and gray matter exploded behind. He pulled his sword and jumped off before the urzyk fell to the ground like a large timber, shaking the area. He stood before the falling monsters as he gave his sword a final swing. The prowess he displayed was too great for a young man to hold. He dominated the fight and ended it before they could even marvel at his skills. He looked small in the distance, but his presence was looming. “Don’t think he’s a rank-S.” Guthered muttered with a dry smile as a bead of sweat fell from his forehead. “I don’t know… he said he’s just a traveler,” Welma answered in a trance. There’s so much to take in about this battle. How can a young man ever find the power to confront a beast that powerful? But no answer dawned on them, and all they could do was marvel at the fight replaying in their minds, hoping to gain some insight. But a shout cut off their connection and brought them back! “Whoo hooh… we won. Sir Kroft, we won! Dear adventurers we won!” Mr Rhodis jumped in celebration as he shook the Krofts, who laughed at his antics. “I’m really thankful we got on him to stay with us. But…” Kroft clenched his eyes together with his fist. His eyes burned with fervent courage. Gguugugu…. Aghh! That got me pumping to fight something. For the next encounter, I’ll face them alone!” “No! Let me have it!” Gunther peeked from the roof, not wanting to be left out. A crazed determination swirled behind his eyes. “It’s better we didn’t find anything,” “Yeah… you guys stop jinxing. Mili argued, which solicited support from Welma. The party turned rowdy.
