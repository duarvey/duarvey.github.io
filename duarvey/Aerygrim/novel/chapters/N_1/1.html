The Traveler [Part 1]

Chapter 1

It’s been a long time since the path running through the mountain range ever had a visitor. It’s been common knowledge that monsters burrowed and thrived in this forest. There was a time when adventurers would take the job as guards, fending off the monsters along the way. An arduous path but manageable nonetheless. The story changed when the wyvern showed up and nested on the peaks. Countless ambushes from the avian beasts raised the risk higher than expected, to the point that the travelers just gave up the thought of even coming near here. After all, it’s not every time the dice will roll in their favor. Settlements on each side of the mountain range suffered economic damage. Trades crippled and villages suffered. Unable to look away from this problem, Marquis Fallarch and the Adventurer’s Guild set out on an expedition to strike at the root of the problem. The glory of liberating the peak… Ended in defeat. The number of wyverns and their power far exceeded the coalition’s expectations; the battle they came for reduced to a feast for the monsters. It was one of the greatest tragedies recorded, and no expedition followed since then. Time passed; unattended monsters- goblins, corpse eaters —creatures that instinctively crave the flesh of man, thrived. Words say this forest became the sole retreat for any outcast of society. Bandits burrowed here as they committed crimes against surrounding settlements, away from the prying eyes of authorities. Everyone assumed the path would be forgotten as time went on. That’s why the news that came to the marquisate came as a shock: ‘The Wyverns' nest was annihilated!’ Like wildfire, the news spread to different regions. Villages and towns rejoiced. Decades of stagnation came to an end as travelers flowed through the mountain pass. The abandoned path once again welcomed its visitors… And so the monsters were more than happy to greet them. [BOOOOOM] Flare shot up from the midst of the trees. The explosion shook the forest's tranquility as it echoed into the sky overlooking the mountain range. Below its trailing red smoke was the clash of blades, mixed with angry shouts of men. Before the parked carriage, where its horse lay motionless and indifferent to the noise, a party of adventurers clashed against a pack of goblins. It was a creature with the height and intelligence of a child—a monster at the bottom tier of monsters that even ordinary people can kill effortlessly. However, these creatures were not proving what people described them to be. “hugahha!” A humongous goblin, a hobgoblin, launched forward, swinging its sword at the waiting shield of a paladin adventurer. The paladin angled his shield to mitigate the impact, yet another dent was added to its rough, dull surface. The club slid past his shield, causing the goblin warrior to lose balance forward. A broken stance. The opening paladin waited to thrust his mana-imbued mace at the undefended warrior. The goblin warrior was hurled into the distance and slid across the ground. It forced itself up as the paladin strode closer to finish the job. An arrow flew past him and stopped him in his tracks. He turned to the origin. Three goblin-archers hiding in the cover of thickets and trees fired their arrows towards him. The paladin raised his shield, bouncing the arrows off. The goblin-warrior got up. Molars ground on each other. Its intention was clear to return the favor from its seething eyes. With the paladin’s eye obscured by the shield, the creature moved across the flying arrows and managed to slip to his side. He noticed too late; the paladin attempted to defend with his shield at the last second. But with an unrefined stance— he was hurled and crashed into a tree. Coughing, he threw his helmet and gasped for air. Blood trickled down his forehead. "fuck..." The paladin fought his heavy eyelids to keep them from dropping. Its vengeance was successful, raising the morale of the other goblins who had yet to have their turn. They charged in with their improvised stone axe, not missing the chance to kill the human that killed several of their kin. But before they even crossed the road— The leading goblin was shot by an arrow dead on the head. The two remaining goblins turned in opposite directions but immediately got sniped in the same vital spot. “Gunther, get up!” An archer bellowed from the rooftop of the carriage situated a little distance to his right. She had a petite build suited for dexterity, with blond hair tied up in a tail. She instinctively leaned sideways with a graceful motion, evading an arrow to her head. She cocked another arrow and fired towards the shooter, piercing its head. Another arrow nocked, and she shifted her target towards the approaching goblin warrior eager to finish her friend. A series of arrows failed to penetrate with the swipe of its sword, felling the arrows in mid-air. Damn! This warrior is a pain in the ass! Far behind, another pair of goblin warriors had yet to make their move, guarding a goblin-warlock who kept their own mage in check. This group seems to be just watching their struggles against their foot soldiers. Are they underestimating them, the adventurers who are prime hunters of their kind? For her party to have been looked down on from a distance by these lowest-tier monsters— What arrogance! The archer kept the momentum with fury, taking away the attention of the goblin warrior. A similar tactic from the warrior, the swordsman sneaked to its rear and delivered a solid slash from his broadsword. Blood spurted like a fountain before the goblin warrior dropped to the ground. “Keep it together, guys. They'll retreat eventually.” He grunted with labored breath. Looks like no one’s around this area. We have to deal with them ourselves if we want to get out of here alive. His brown hair stuck to his sweat-moistened face. Light armor, battered and soaked with red stains, showed how hard he had been fighting. No one has yet noticed the distress flare they sent. With the forest path opened, surely there’s a traveler or two who will rush to their rescue. But it seemed they had to hold on for much longer. In the far distance from the road, the goblin-warlock finally stepped up. Embers ignited at the tip of its staff. The swordsman dashed towards the carriage, with rearguards in mind, leaving the paladin behind. Even immobile, the man can protect himself with the inherent holy barrier, and the barricade of trees will protect him from the blast. It was up to him to defend the rearguards, who had better skill in dealing with spells. The swordsman froze on the spot, eyes darting at the blazing projectile. The witch was not idle and was already expecting it. Her staff glowed with mana, summoning a barrier seconds before the fireball hit. An explosion erupted, lighting up the surroundings. Hot air lingered as embers burned the surrounding weeds. Obscured by the smoke, both forces stopped. They waited patiently for the smoke and the heat to recede. In the lull of the battlefield, the surviving goblin-archer tread closer, hiding behind the trees as it moved. It peeked from the trunk before coming out of its hiding spot. He let loose its arrow— it pierced through the smoke and found its target. “Uagh!” The witch’s shoulder bled. “Mili! Are you alright?” The archer cried. “You’re dead. You bastard!” Exacting revenge, she let loose her arrow and planted it through the grinning archer’s head. “Mili! Hold on!” She went down from the top of the carriage and checked her friend. The Witch’s face contorted in pain as she gripped the arrow. But the archer warned strongly. “Don’t pull the arrow.” She said before she returned her attention forward. You fuckers! She vehemently cursed them in her head. The goblin-warlock’s fang bared; eyes glinted with malice. No more mage will block the spell and protect the carriage, and the other protector is missing. A chance they’ve all been waiting for. The goblins encroached closer with wicked grins. goblin-warlock barked an order to the goblin warrior on its side before it recited another spell. The swordsman glanced back with troubled eyes. As much as he wanted to come to the witch’s rescue, he didn’t have the option, as he needed to guard the front. With the paladin still gathering his strength with healing magic, it fell solely on him to protect the party. “Shit… This is not good.” the swordsman cursed as he looked back at the carriage. With no one to stop the fireball, defeat was imminent. He could somehow stop it if he used mana to cut down the spell. But what came after was an exhausted swordsman against many goblins. The swordsman bit his lip as he resolved himself. He barked an order. But before he could— “Kroft, take everyone out of here! I’ll hold them off!” The paladin walked toward them and finally dragged his battered body to the group. “I can somehow block its spell and buy some time.” He hadn’t forgotten his role as the tank of his party until the very end. But looking at his stature, it seemed he was in no proper condition for longer fights. “No! I’ll fight. It’s my duty as the leader.” The swordsman stepped forward, not wanting to be outdone, and brandished his weapon. He was resolute in fighting alongside his friend to protect the rest. “Hey!” “Welma, take Mili and the family away from here.” “…” Welma froze up at the order. Perhaps he heard what the swordsman said; a distressed voice of a man escaped from the carriage window. “Adventurers, is everything alright!?” “Sorry, Sir, but you have to get away from here.” The swordsman had nothing to offer his client but a pained apology. The situation is bad enough that they have to take a realistic approach. “Welma, get them to safety! I’m counting on you.” The swordsman said without removing his eyes from the goblins. Behind him, the archer stared in, dazed. It was less of a command but more of a plea. Adventurers make a living by delving themselves into danger. Deaths were not uncommon in this profession. They’ve evaded death countless times. But it seems it’s time for their turn. This situation is beyond salvation. Someone has to stay for the other to leave. Even that is a gamble if they can outrun the goblins. She understood what fate awaited them. She bit her lip in frustration. She had no choice. She hesitantly tucked her bow on her back and carried out the last order of her captain. The archer, Welma, lifted her injured friend. But she froze up after a few steps. The surroundings got brighter. The Fireball, twice the size of the previous one, flew forth from the goblin-warlock. The paladin and the swordsman brandished their weapons. Mana surged from their inner core, imbuing their weapons. Eyes gleamed with intent as they waited for the spell within their reach. But something cut in the air with blinding speed. The fireball was snuffed out in mid-air before it could even reach them. ““…”” The adventurers froze on the spot, dumbfounded. Even the encroaching goblins halted their track, unable to make out what happened. It was supposed to be the opening they were waiting for. Another failure? The goblin-warlock snapped out of its daze. Enraged, he stepped forward, pushing aside the remaining goblin warrior guarding him. Its shoulder shook in frustration. The goblin-warlock snapped and pointed its staff. Mana gathered once again, and the summoned embers blazed into an orb. But before it could fully develop, the caster’s head burst open— Fell with a dull thud. Not giving time to react, the goblin warrior beside him suffered the same fate; its head exploded on its side. This mushy explosion was rather out of place on their side, so a goblin looked behind only to see the respected warlock and warrior lay there motionless. He pointed behind and cried to its kin. But a second later, it was his turn to drop. A headshot. Deadly accurate. Based on the direction of the burst, someone is definitely shooting at them. But before they could look, it was their turn to die. They have a new enemy. And it’s something more dangerous than the adventurers before them. Instinct kicked in; the goblins retreated one step slowly before they burst into running after one goblin dropped dead. But even then, projectiles never stopped, as they reduced their numbers even further. Corpses piled up, staining the fresh sapling with blood and gore. With the figure of the last goblin vanished in the depths of the forest, silence spread once more. A contrast to the noisy thoughts swirling in the swordsman’s head. We survived… but What was that? Who was that? Words had yet to escape from his mouth or his friends’, but the same questions uninanimously etched on their faces. They all turned towards the origin. But there was truly no one there. Only the empty path with passing breeze rustling the trees. This seizing silence stretched for quite a while longer until— “You guys alright?” A youthful voice came from behind, and released them from their binds. Caught off guard, the warriors raised their weapons haphazardly as they turned around and jumped back. A person in a white coat, coupled with a mask over his face. But the youthful voice gave away his young age. “You guys still lively, it seems. Except for the mage.” What is this kid!? I never sensed him until he spoke! Kroft the swordsman screamed caution in his head. For a swordsman who honed his senses through discipline, to just fail to detect someone near him just means one thing. Once again, no one seemed to utter a word. Perhaps they were busy sorting their thoughts. But it would be rude not to say something to their savior, so the Kroft snapped out of his thoughts. He was about to open his mouth when— "You better treat her quickly." The young man fetched a vial from his breast pocket and threw it to Welma. "This is a high-grade potion…" The archer whispered in surprise. She turned to the young man and nodded. "We are grateful." She brought Mili down and tended her wounds. Kroft realised his sword was still drawn to their savior, and awkwardly put it back into its sheath. The paladin relaxed his stance as well. “Many thanks, traveler. You really saved us there. And sorry for impudence earlier.” The swordsman wryly laughed and bowed down, but with utter caution. “It’s alright, its a complete valid warrior response.” His coat was unseemly clean despite traveling deep in the forest— No… There was a cut. This person didn't look like an adventurer, but more like someone who belonged to the upper caste of society. But what is a kid like him doing out here? “Is it over?” A wary man alighted from the carriage and approached the group. “Y-yes, Mr. Rhodis. Thank Sinafel this lad is here with us.” The paladin said. “He’s the one who finished off the rest of the goblins.” “I see… thank the goddess indeed. Thank you so much, dear traveler.” Rhodis bowed in gratitude, his forehead moist with sweat. “Think nothing of it.” The young traveler shrugged. “I must say, I’m surprised that there’s still someone using this path. I thought I was alone here.” “Circumstance pushed us to use this road." Mr. Rhodis started, ”Oh right. My name is Rhodis. A merchant. My family and I are on our way to Lefora right now.” “The fortified city?” Asked the young man. He turned his gaze to the furthest end of the forest path and said, “So this is where it leads to.” “You came here without knowing?" “I’m just letting myself be dragged by my feet. I don't care where I’m going.” The young man said playfully. "I-I see." “Mr. Rhodis, I think we can continue these pleasantries on the way. With these corpses lying around, this draws the nose of corpse eaters.” Korft said gravely, looking at his surroundings. Humanoid creatures with hideous faces peeked from the trees. They looked like malnourished, corpse-like undead but without the death aura they usually have. These are one of the scavengers of the forest that eat corpses, such as the name corpse eater. Dealing with them is easy but a hassle, as these creatures reek of a foul smell much worse than a goblin. They were probably waiting for the adventurers to leave so they could feast on the goblin. "R-right," Rhodis said, tense as he looked at the corpse eater staring at them. “W-why don’t you come with us, Sire? I’m ashamed to admit, but there might still be some monster that may prove to be too difficult for us. Don’t worry about the gold; I’ll properly compensate you, this one as well.” Rhodis offered. A hint of concern was etched on Kroft’s face. With this fight’s outcome, he would have a hard time going forward. With this forest famed for its dangers, there might be other monsters waiting for them ahead. What chance of survival would they have if they faced a monster far stronger than the one they struggled against earlier? Not to mention the damage they sustained earlier; the prospect of defeat stacks heavily in their favor. Unless this young man has to escort them. Though they still have to convince the person in question. “My kindness is not for sale, Mr. Rhodis.” The young man shrugged. “But I’ll welcome the ride. I’m getting tired of walking too. But..." The young man turned to the motionless horse. “Your horse is busted.” "It’s... okay. I just put them to sleep so they won’t panic." Mili approached with an awkward smile. It seemed the potion recovered her strength considerably as she walked straight with no visible discomfort. She tapped her staff on the ground and muttered, "Awake." The horse stirred and slowly rose on their feet. "Wow." “Looks like your concern was unfounded, Sire.” “R-right! Let us depart.” Ushered by Mr. Rhodis, they all alighted from the carriage and resumed their journey along the forest path.
